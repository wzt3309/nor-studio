# NOR Studio

NOR Studio (AND, OR, NOT) is a personal product practice repository and a record of progress toward becoming a one-person company (OPC).

It develops product judgment, UI and interaction design, full-stack engineering, and collaboration with coding agents through small, self-contained products. Reasoning, decisions, intermediate artifacts, and reflections help later projects test and refine earlier lessons.

See the [repository layout](docs/structure.md) for project structure, design artifacts, shared knowledge, and build conventions.

## Background

The initial motivation was to develop taste in UI and interaction design. Practicing the full journey from idea to delivery reveals the dependencies and tradeoffs between stages.

Recurring challenges include:

- Explaining why an interface feels wrong and giving Codex useful feedback.
- Choosing between analysis, a plan, a prototype, and implementation, and knowing what to decide first.
- Delegating both reasoning and execution when unsure, then repeatedly correcting unexpected results.
- Finding references that expose blind spots and unfamiliar methods or tools.
- Reducing unproductive iteration, using time and tokens well, and understanding when different approaches and stacks fit.

Product outcomes, the building process, and changes in personal judgment all contribute to the learning.

## Goals

The long-term goal is to build and deliver products independently as an OPC, with particular attention to judgment when working with agents.

| Area | Judgment to develop |
| --- | --- |
| Product | Define users, problems, requirements, and scope from real situations; choose the next question to validate. |
| Design | Turn vague impressions into observable problems, understand visual and interaction tradeoffs, and develop taste and evaluation criteria. |
| Agent collaboration | Decide what to clarify, delegate, request as evidence, accept, and correct. |
| Engineering | Choose tools and stacks for the situation, accounting for implementation cost, feedback speed, and later consequences. |
| Reflection | Use conversations, decisions, and artifacts to explain deviations and identify improvements to test next time. |

## Product Practice

Each practice addresses a real use case through a self-contained product and an explicit learning objective. Scope and polish follow that objective; a phase can conclude once there is enough feedback.

Practice covers requirements, product and interaction design, UI, implementation, validation, and delivery. Reflection accompanies important decisions and phase endings. Retain the original problem, chosen approach, influential intermediate artifacts, usage outcomes, and intended adjustments.

## Reflection

Reflection focuses on the work shared by a person and Codex. Conversations, drafts, prototypes, code, and final artifacts provide evidence; the person supplies or confirms their own intent, hesitation, and judgment.

A useful retrospective asks:

- What were the goal, existing judgments, and uncertainties?
- What was requested from Codex, and what did it deliver?
- Which conversation, artifact, or external evidence changed a decision?
- Did a deviation arise in requirements, design, delegation, implementation, or acceptance? What evidence is missing?
- What cheaper action could have exposed the issue earlier? Which discarded explorations reduced important uncertainty?
- What will change next time, and how will its effect be observed?

Lessons remain provisional as later practice reveals their limits. Record the context, reasoning, and experienced costs of tool and stack choices.
